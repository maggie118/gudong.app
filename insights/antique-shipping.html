快递摔碎一只乾隆碗？古董运输包装的8层防护法

Smashed Qianlong bowl in transit? The 8-layer packing method for antiques

运

古董运输最怕的不是"远"，而是"包装没做好"。一枚乾隆碗从新加坡寄到吉隆坡，中途可能经历 5 次以上装卸。下面这套 8 层防护法，是本地藏家、拍卖行常用的标准做法。

先说明：本文为收藏科普，不构成修复操作指导。高价值器物建议委托专业艺术品物流公司，不要自行打包。

第 1 层：无酸纸包裹器物本体

先用无酸纸（或专用棉纸）包裹器物本体，避免器物与后续材料直接接触。书画、纸制品尤其重要。

第 2 层：气泡膜（气泡朝外）

气泡膜的气泡面朝外，光滑面贴器物。这样既能缓冲，又不会在器物表面留下"气泡印"。

第 3 层：泡沫板护角

器物四角、口沿、把手等最脆弱的位置，用泡沫板或珍珠棉加固，防止局部受力。

第 4 层：悬浮包装

把包裹好的器物悬空固定在箱体中央，四周留出缓冲空间。这也是专业艺术品物流的核心逻辑。

第 5 层：内箱

第一层硬箱，通常用瓦楞纸箱。器物在内箱中不应有任何晃动。

第 6 层：外箱

第二层硬箱，通常比内箱大 5–10 厘米，两层之间填充缓冲材料。双层箱可以吸收运输中的冲击。

第 7 层：防震与方向标识

箱体外部贴"易碎""向上""勿压"标识，并用箭头标明方向。同时在外箱顶部留"开箱方向"提示，避免收件人误开。

第 8 层：留证

这是最容易被忽略、也最重要的一层。打包全过程建议拍照或录像：

  • 器物现状（多角度）
  • 每一层包装过程
  • 封箱前的整体照
  • 快递单号、保价金额、收件信息
提醒：高价值器物建议保价，并选择支持艺术品运输的物流公司。普通快递的"保价"往往不等于"足额赔付"。

不同材质的特别提醒

  • 瓷器：口沿、底足、把手是受力点，需要重点加固
  • 书画：卷装优于平放；避免折痕与挤压
  • 木器：避免高湿环境；长途运输建议加干燥剂
  • 铜器：避免与酸性材料接触；单独包裹
  • 玉器：小件容易丢失，建议放在硬盒内再装入大箱

常见误区

  • 误区一：报纸包瓷器——油墨会污染器物
  • 误区二：只包一层气泡膜就寄——缓冲远远不够
  • 误区三：器物在箱内可以晃动——这是破损的主要原因
  • 误区四：不拍照留证——出问题无法索赔

下一步可以做什么？

  • 提前准备：无酸纸、气泡膜、泡沫板、双层纸箱、缓冲填充
  • 重要器物委托专业艺术品物流，不要用普通快递
  • 打包全程录像，作为纠纷时的证据

The real risk in shipping antiques is not distance — it is poor packing. A Qianlong bowl sent from Singapore to Kuala Lumpur may be handled five or more times. Here is the 8-layer method used by local collectors and auction houses.

Note: This article is for educational reference only. For high-value objects, use a specialist art logistics company rather than packing yourself.

Layer 1: Acid-free paper around the object

Wrap the object first in acid-free or conservation tissue, to keep it from contacting other materials. Especially important for paintings and paper.

Layer 2: Bubble wrap (bubbles outward)

Bubbles face out, smooth side against the object. This cushions without leaving bubble marks on the surface.

Layer 3: Foam corner protection

Corners, rims, and handles — the weakest points — get foam or EPE reinforcement to prevent localised pressure.

Layer 4: Suspension packing

Suspend the wrapped object in the centre of the box, with buffer space all around. This is the core logic of professional art logistics.

Layer 5: Inner box

First rigid box, usually corrugated. The object should not move at all inside.

Layer 6: Outer box

Second rigid box, 5–10 cm larger, with cushioning material between. Double-boxing absorbs in-transit shock.

Layer 7: Shock and orientation labels

Mark "Fragile", "This way up", "Do not crush" on the outside, with directional arrows. Also add an "open here" mark on top so the receiver does not open the wrong side.

Layer 8: Evidence

The most overlooked and most important layer. Photograph or video the whole packing process:

  • The object's condition (multiple angles)
  • Each packing step
  • The final sealed box
  • Tracking number, insured value, recipient details
Note: For high-value objects, insure and choose a carrier that handles art. Ordinary courier "insurance" often does not equal full-value compensation.

Material-specific reminders

  • Porcelain: rim, foot, and handles are stress points — reinforce
  • Paintings: rolled is better than flat; avoid creases and compression
  • Wood: avoid high humidity; add desiccant for long routes
  • Bronze: avoid acidic materials; wrap separately
  • Jade: small pieces get lost — use a hard case inside the larger box

Common misconceptions

  • Newspaper around porcelain — ink contaminates the object
  • One layer of bubble wrap is enough — cushioning is far from enough
  • It can move inside the box — this is the main cause of breakage
  • No photo record — no basis for a claim

What to do next

  • Prepare: acid-free paper, bubble wrap, foam board, double boxes, filler
  • For important objects, use professional art logistics, not ordinary couriers
  • Record the whole packing process on video as evidence
