新加坡瓷器市场：去哪看、怎么看

Singapore porcelain market: where to look, how to look

2026-01-28 · 6 分钟阅读6 min read · 古董集编辑整理Gudong editorial
MARKET / 市场观察 MARKET / Guide 新加坡瓷器市场 Singapore porcelain market 去哪看 · 怎么看 Where to look · how to look
瓷

新加坡不大，但看瓷器的渠道其实不少。这篇是写给本地藏家的实用地图：五种渠道，各自的定位、适合谁、要小心什么。

先说明：本文是新加坡本地瓷器购买渠道的概览，不构成投资建议。具体店铺、拍卖行请自行核实营业信息。

五个主要渠道

01
古董店 · Antique Shops

实体古董店

主要集中在 Tan Boon Liat Building、Tanglin、Dempsey 一带。货真价实的高端店有，鱼龙混杂的也有。适合已经有一定眼力的人。

适合：进阶藏家 小心：高价低质、故事包装
02
拍卖行 · Auction Houses

本地拍卖行

新加坡有几家常年做中国艺术品的拍卖行。预展免费开放，是最好的“上手学习”机会。适合想建立标准感的新手。

适合：所有阶段的藏家 小心：图录描述 ≠ 实物
03
跳蚤市场 · Flea Markets

跳蚤市场

如 Sungei Road（已关闭）、China Square 周末市场。真品少，捡漏机会也少，但价格低，适合练眼力。

适合：练手、找小件 小心：仿品、做旧、来路不明
04
线上 · Online

线上平台

Carousell、Facebook 群组、国际拍卖平台。方便但风险最高，图片可以 P，照片看不到釉面和手感。

适合：有经验、能远程判断 小心：照片造假、货不对板
05
藏家社群 · Collector Circles

本地藏家社群

通过协会、微信/WhatsApp 群、拍卖行活动认识。信息最真实，但需要时间积累信任。

适合：长期玩家 小心：“圈内价”也有水分

五个渠道对比

渠道 价格 风险 适合
实体古董店高中进阶
本地拍卖行中-高低所有阶段
跳蚤市场低高练手
线上平台低-中最高有经验
藏家社群中低长期玩家
实战提醒：新手最快的成长路径，是先去拍卖预展上手真品，再去跳蚤市场练习判断。顺序反了，容易建立错误的眼力。

怎么用这五个渠道？

  1. 先看真品：拍卖预展是免费的真品教室
  2. 再练眼力：跳蚤市场便宜，适合练判断
  3. 再建网络：加入藏家社群，长期积累信息
  4. 最后出手：有把握时再进实体店和拍卖

常见误区

  • 误区一：拍卖行不会卖假——拍卖行也走眼
  • 误区二：跳蚤市场能捡漏——捡漏概率极低
  • 误区三：线上便宜——便宜往往有原因
  • 误区四：熟人不会骗——熟人也要看东西

下一步可以做什么？

  • 查一下本月的拍卖预展时间，先去上手看真品
  • 周末去一个跳蚤市场，只练眼力，不急着买
  • 加一两个本地藏家群，先潜水学习

Singapore is small, but there are plenty of channels for seeing porcelain. This is a practical map for local collectors: five channels, their positioning, who they suit, and what to watch out for.

Note: This is an overview of local channels, not investment advice. Check shop and auction details yourself.

Five main channels

01
Antique Shops

Brick-and-mortar shops

Mainly around Tan Boon Liat Building, Tanglin, Dempsey. Some are high-end and genuine; others mix good and bad. Best for those with some eye.

Suits: Advanced collectors Watch: High price, low quality, story selling
02
Auction Houses

Local auction houses

Several do Chinese art year-round. Previews are free and the best place to handle real pieces. Great for beginners building a standard.

Suits: All levels Watch: Catalogue ≠ reality
03
Flea Markets

Flea markets

e.g. Sungei Road (now closed), China Square weekend market. Few real pieces, few bargains, but cheap for practice.

Suits: Practice, small items Watch: Fakes, ageing, unclear provenance
04
Online

Online platforms

Carousell, Facebook groups, international auction sites. Convenient but highest risk — photos lie, glaze and feel can't be judged.

Suits: Experienced buyers Watch: Fake photos, mismatched goods
05
Collector Circles

Local collector circles

Via associations, chat groups, auction events. The most honest information, but takes time to build trust.

Suits: Long-term players Watch: "Inside price" isn't always good

Channel comparison

Channel Price Risk Suits
Antique shopsHighMediumAdvanced
Auction housesMed-HighLowAll levels
Flea marketsLowHighPractice
OnlineLow-MedHighestExperienced
Collector circlesMediumLowLong-term
Tip: The fastest path for a beginner: preview real pieces at auctions first, then practice at flea markets. Reverse the order and you'll build the wrong eye.

How to use them

  1. See real: auction previews are a free classroom
  2. Practice: flea markets are cheap for judgement
  3. Network: join collector circles for real info
  4. Buy: go to shops and auctions once confident

Common misconceptions

  • Auction = no fakes — they miss too
  • Flea market = bargains — rare
  • Online = cheaper — usually for a reason
  • Friends = safe — check the piece anyway
