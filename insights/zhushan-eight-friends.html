Through History · No. 6

一群画家，把瓷器变成了画布

A group of painters turned porcelain into canvas

民国景德镇，他们不画碗，画的是自己的心情

In Republican Jingdezhen, they painted not for the bowl, but for themselves

1928 — 1930s
2026-03-152026-03-15 · 4 分钟阅读4 min read · 古董圈编辑部 · 未经授权禁止转载Gudong Editorial · No reposting without permission

珠山，是景德镇市中心的一座小山。明清两代，御窑厂就设在这里。

1928 年，一群画家在这座小山附近聚了起来。他们不烧官窑，也不做大路货，只画一种东西：瓷板。

他们就是后来被称为"珠山八友"的一批人。

I

民国景德镇，跌到了谷底

1911 年清朝灭亡，御窑厂停烧。景德镇一下子失去了最大的客户。

加上战乱、洋瓷冲击、海运中断——20 世纪初的景德镇，可以说是三百年来最萧条的时候。

但就在这个时候，一群画家聚到了一起。

❦ ❦ ❦
II

"八友"是哪八个人

严格来说，"珠山八友"不是固定八人。它是一个松散的画家群体，前后加入过十几个人。

最常被提到的有：王琦、王大凡、汪野亭、邓碧珊、毕伯涛、何许人、程意亭、刘雨岑。

发起人是王琦。他早年是捏面人的，后来改画瓷板，擅长人物画。他学的是清代画家黄慎的风格，线条简练，人物神态生动。

其他人各有所长：王大凡画人物，汪野亭画山水，邓碧珊画鱼，毕伯涛画花鸟，何许人专画雪景。

他们每月聚会一次，各带作品，互相题字、品评。这种"文人雅集"的方式，在景德镇是第一次。

珠山八友的真正贡献，不是发明了某种工艺，而是把"画家"这个身份，带进了景德镇。
III

他们画的是什么

在他们之前，景德镇的画工大多是在"画瓷器"——画缠枝莲、龙凤、八宝，都是批量生产的固定图案。

珠山八友画的是"画"——山水、人物、花鸟，每一幅都有构图、有留白、有题诗、有印章。他们不是在装饰一个器物，而是在一件器物上创作一幅画。

用的技法，叫新粉彩。

新粉彩是在传统粉彩的基础上，吸收了中国画的没骨法和渲染法。它比老粉彩更接近纸绢画，颜色更柔和，层次更丰富，能在瓷板上做出水墨画的效果。

更重要的：他们在瓷板上题字、用印。这在以前是文人画的特权，现在搬到了瓷器上。

一块瓷板，变成了文人可以"写画"的媒介。

IV

为什么他们今天这么贵

珠山八友的瓷板画，现在在拍卖行是硬通货。一幅王琦的人物瓷板，可以卖到几百万人民币。王大凡、汪野亭的作品也都在百万级别。

为什么这么贵？三个原因：

一、产量少。他们不是工厂，是手工作坊。每人一辈子能画的瓷板，可能也就几百幅。加上战乱损毁，完整的存世量很少。

二、身份特殊。他们是从"匠人"变成"画家"的第一代。这种身份转换，在艺术史上非常罕见。买他们的作品，等于买了一段景德镇的现代史。

三、审美独立。他们画的不是订单，是自己的心境。山水里寄托着逃禅之意，人物里带着文人的诙谐，花鸟里有闲适。这种"为自己而画"的气质，在瓷器史上独一无二。

到今天，珠山八友已经成了一个流派。很多当代瓷画家在追他们的路子，但那种民国文人的味道，再也画不出来了。

民国是中国最动荡的年代之一。

但就是在这个年代，一群景德镇的画家，把瓷器从工艺品变成了艺术品。

他们留下的不是某种技法，而是一种态度——

画东西的人，也可以有自己的名字。

Zhushan is a small hill in the centre of Jingdezhen. For two dynasties, the imperial kilns stood here.

In 1928, a group of painters gathered around that hill. They did not fire imperial wares or mass-market goods — they painted one thing: porcelain plaques.

These were the painters who would come to be called the "Zhushan Eight Friends".

I

Republican Jingdezhen Hit Rock Bottom

When the Qing fell in 1911, the imperial kilns shut down. Jingdezhen lost its biggest customer overnight.

Add to that war, foreign porcelain imports, and disrupted shipping — early twentieth-century Jingdezhen was arguably at its lowest point in three centuries.

And yet, at precisely this moment, a group of painters came together.

II

Who Were the "Eight Friends"?

Strictly speaking, the "Zhushan Eight Friends" were never a fixed group. It was a loose circle; over the years more than a dozen painters joined.

The most often cited are Wang Qi, Wang Dafan, Wang Yeting, Deng Bishan, Bi Botao, He Xuren, Cheng Yiting, and Liu Yucen.

Wang Qi was the initiator. He began as a dough-figure sculptor, then turned to porcelain plaques, specialising in figures. He followed the style of the Qing painter Huang Shen — spare lines, vivid expressions.

The others each had their speciality: Wang Dafan on figures, Wang Yeting on landscape, Deng Bishan on fish, Bi Botao on birds and flowers, He Xuren on snow scenes.

They met once a month, bringing their work, adding inscriptions and comments for each other. This "literati gathering" format was a first in Jingdezhen.

The real contribution of the Zhushan Eight Friends was not a technique — it was bringing the identity of the "painter" into Jingdezhen.
III

What They Painted

Before them, most Jingdezhen painters were "painting porcelain" — lotus scrolls, dragons, phoenixes, eight treasures. All fixed patterns, mass produced.

The Eight Friends painted "paintings" — landscapes, figures, birds, flowers. Every piece had composition, negative space, an inscribed poem, a seal. They were not decorating an object; they were creating a painting on an object.

Their technique was called new famille rose.

New famille rose absorbed the "boneless" and wash techniques of Chinese painting. It was closer to paper-and-silk painting than older famille rose — softer colours, richer layering, capable of ink-wash effects on porcelain plaques.

More importantly: they inscribed text and used seals. That had once been the privilege of literati painters. Now it moved onto porcelain.

A porcelain plaque became a medium a literatus could "write and paint" on.

IV

Why They Are So Valuable Today

Porcelain plaques by the Zhushan Eight Friends are now blue-chip at auction. A Wang Qi figure plaque can fetch several million RMB. Wang Dafan and Wang Yeting pieces also reach million-RMB levels.

Why so expensive? Three reasons:

First, scarcity. They were a workshop, not a factory. Over a lifetime, each might paint only a few hundred plaques. With wartime losses, intact survivals are few.

Second, status. They were the first generation to move from "craftsman" to "painter". This identity shift is rare in art history. Buying their work is buying a piece of Jingdezhen's modern history.

Third, aesthetic independence. They were not painting to order — they were painting their own mood. Landscape as refuge, figures with a scholar's wit, birds and flowers with quiet ease. That "painting for oneself" quality is unique in the history of porcelain.

Today the Zhushan Eight Friends have become a school. Many contemporary porcelain painters follow their path. But the flavour of Republican literati — that cannot be painted again.

The Republican era was one of China's most turbulent.

But in that era, a group of Jingdezhen painters turned porcelain from craft into art.

What they left behind was not a technique, but an attitude —

that the person who paints can have a name.
